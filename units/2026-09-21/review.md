# CSC110: Propositional logic, negation, and predicates — review and guidance

Unofficial student study notes. Consult official course materials for current requirements. Practice questions are not official assessments.

## Quick revision checklist

- Explain why OR includes the both-true case, while AND requires it.
- Identify the whole hypothesis and conclusion of an implication. Its only false case is a true hypothesis with a false conclusion.
- Distinguish the equivalent contrapositive from the generally non-equivalent converse. One differing truth-table row disproves equivalence.
- Translate `⇒` as `not p or q` and Boolean `⇔` as `p == q`.
- Negate a conjunction/disjunction by negating both parts and switching AND/OR. Remove double negations.
- Use `¬(H ⇒ C) ≡ H ∧ ¬C`; negate a biconditional by describing its two unequal-value cases.
- Preserve scope: *not all* is not *none*, and negating a whole conjunction is not negating just its first part.
- Break inequality chains into adjacent comparisons before negating; retain equality in the correct complement.
- Define a predicate's input domain and distinguish its inputs from its Boolean output.

## Instructor guidance and answer requirements

- Translate as literally as possible for now. Preserve the difference between *not all dogs are fluffy* and *all dogs are not fluffy*. The instructor explicitly said the latter is not a correct replacement and would lose marks in the discussed test/exam translation. *At least one dog is not fluffy* was accepted.
- When the exercise asks for simplification, push negations as far inside as possible. For negated biconditionals, expand the connective rather than stop at another biconditional.
- Give the requested English translation as well as the simplified formula. A translation of a negated biconditional must include both alternative cases.
- Established equivalences may be reused for compound formulas in exercises, assignments, and tests. Treat the entire hypothesis and conclusion as the two parts of the rule.
- The instructor recommends truth tables for checking uncertain equivalences: compare the results for the same assignments.
- **Official reading requirement — 3.2 Predicate Logic:** always include the input domain when defining a predicate. This is guidance from the notes, not an additional spoken instruction.
